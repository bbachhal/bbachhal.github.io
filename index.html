Where I've worked

The Renatural

Brooklyn, NY
Jul – Aug 2026
Engineering Intern, Robotics & Manufacturing

I showed up to a bare patch of floor and left behind a Universal Robots UR12e cell running production. Twenty-two custom components, a fifty-piece frame, and a process that used to need someone reaching into a machine.

  • Cut cycle time from 90 minutes to 45–60 by replacing a manual-intervention 5-axis process.
  • Designed end-effector mounts for a gripper and a proprietary injection head. Compliance in that mount becomes tip error, and it compounds across 50–100 arc injections per part.
  • Wired Keyence light curtains and E-stops into the robot safety input — then walked every access point and broke each one by hand, because a schematic isn't a test.
  • Three 5-axis workholding fixtures, sized with a worst-case stack-up. Yield up about 20%, and the operator no longer has to enter the envelope.
  • Characterized a natural fiber that had no datasheet and no published tolerance. Two weeks with calipers before I could design anything against it.
  • Validated toolpaths in RoboDK, chased Rockwell PLC and relay faults, and presented the process findings to company leadership.

UR12e · RoboDK · fixture design · tolerance stack-up · 5-axis CNC · Rockwell PLC · machine safety · metrology

The CAD and photos from this one belong to the company and are under NDA, so there's nothing to show. Ask me about it instead — the reasoning is the interesting part anyway.

Deteqt

Sydney, Australia
Jan – Jun 2026
Mechanical Engineering Intern

Enclosure and thermal hardware for a diamond-based quantum magnetometer, a sensor built for navigating where GPS doesn't work. Its accuracy drifts with temperature, which makes thermal a functional requirement, not a survival one.

  • IP65-rated ruggedized housing in SolidWorks, rated for 24 hours in the field above 40 °C.
  • Sealing killed convection at the source, so the problem collapsed to conduction path and interface area. The aluminum baseplate carries structure and heat at once rather than adding mass that only does one job.
  • Four FDM and SLA iterations to production geometry, each one measured rather than eyeballed.
  • Released the full documentation package — fastener standardization, heat-set insert specs, assembly drawings. An outside supplier built it to print and never called me once.

SolidWorks · GD&T · thermal design · DFM · IP65 sealing · drawing packages · FDM/SLA

Also under NDA. Same offer.

I run the mechanical side of BU's autonomous racing team. Right now that means leading the mechanical design on a Guinness World Record attempt to turn a 2008 Corvette into an autonomous platform.

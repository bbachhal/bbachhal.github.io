Shaft Support and Bearing Housing

A structural shaft support and bearing housing designed to rigidly locate a rotating shaft while resisting bending moments and vibration from cantilevered loads. The rib-reinforced body, thickened bore region, and wide mounting base emphasize stiffness, alignment, and manufacturability, making the part suitable for CNC-machined automation, robotics, or drivetrain applications.

Ball Valve Assembly

A mechanical ball-valve assembly designed to regulate fluid flow through rotational actuation of a spherical valve element. The design emphasizes precise sealing surfaces, accurate component fit, and manufacturability, demonstrating fluid control mechanisms, tolerances, and assembly-level design.

Enclosed Gearbox

A compact enclosed gearbox that transmits and reduces rotational motion through an internal gear train inside a rigid bolted enclosure. The design emphasizes shaft alignment, load containment, and serviceable assembly, with reinforced housing, flanged covers, and accessible fasteners suitable for CNC manufacturing.

An external handle drives an internal shaft connected to the gear set, demonstrating torque transmission, bearing support, and mechanical power flow within a sealed system. Enclosure geometry, fastener placement, and modular covers reflect consideration of manufacturability, maintenance, and structural stiffness for industrial and robotic applications.

Four-Bar Linkage

A planar four-bar linkage that converts rotary input motion into a constrained output trajectory through a system of pinned rigid links. The mechanism demonstrates kinematic constraints, link length selection, and joint articulation, all fundamental to motion design in robotics and machinery.

The design emphasizes accurate joint alignment, manufacturable link geometry, and realistic pin interfaces, reflecting careful attention to tolerances, assembly, and repeatable motion. Mechanisms like this appear in robotic arms, suspension systems, and automated tooling wherever predictable motion profiles matter. The project also gave me practical experience with SolidWorks motion study and simulation for analyzing four-bar kinematics.

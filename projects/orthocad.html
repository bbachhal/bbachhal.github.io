OrthoCAD Pro

An advanced orthotic design system that turns biomechanical inputs into patient-specific, manufacturable CAD geometry, without manual CAD iteration.

RoleSole designer and developer
TypePersonal project
TimelineJul 2025 – Sep 2025
LinksTechnical report

Overview and Motivation

OrthoCAD Pro is a personal project I developed to streamline the creation of custom foot orthoses by integrating biomechanical principles, parametric CAD modeling, and manufacturability constraints. The system reduces manual design time while improving consistency, repeatability, and fit accuracy.

It addresses a core engineering challenge: how to encode biomechanical intent, manufacturing constraints, and geometric robustness into a repeatable automated design pipeline, rather than relying on manual CAD iteration.

The motivation was personal. Many of the elders in my family require orthotic soles to walk comfortably. I wanted to build a framework for orthotic design that could be more accessible and produced faster than traditional manufacturing allows.

System Architecture

The system is structured as a multi-stage pipeline, with each stage enforcing engineering constraints to prevent downstream failures.

  • Input acquisition captures foot geometry from a 3D scan or a parameterized foot profile.
  • Design logic maps biomechanical inputs to orthotic features.
  • Parametric CAD generation produces fully constrained orthotic geometry.
  • Manufacturing validation checks thickness, curvature, and material feasibility.

Each stage is deterministic, repeatable, and tolerant of noisy inputs, which reflects the variability of real clinical data.

Parametric CAD Model

The orthotic is a fully parametric model where every major functional feature is driven by explicit design variables rather than manual geometry edits.

ArchHeight and stiffness profile
HeelCup depth and wall angle
PostingMedial and lateral geometry
ForefootThickness and flexibility zones
EnvelopeLength, width, edge taper
BehaviorHierarchically linked parameters

All parameters are hierarchically linked to ensure smooth surface continuity, no self-intersections or invalid solids, and predictable response to parameter changes. That eliminates fragile CAD behavior and allows rapid regeneration of new designs without manual rework.

Biomechanics-Driven Design Logic

Rather than treating the orthotic as a purely geometric shell, I encoded biomechanical intent directly into the design logic. High heel pressure is addressed by increasing heel cup depth and localized thickness. A collapsed arch profile is corrected with an elevated medial arch and a gradual stiffness transition. Forefoot load imbalance is mitigated through asymmetric thickness modulation. The result is an orthotic that is functionally corrective, not just anatomically fitted.

Manufacturing Constraints

A major focus was making sure every generated design is manufacturable without manual intervention. This was the hardest part, and to solve it I consulted local orthotics manufacturing experts. Prothotic Laboratories helped me build a list of constraints that I embedded directly in the design logic.

  • Minimum and maximum wall thickness
  • Curvature limits to prevent print failures
  • Smooth transitions to reduce stress concentrations
  • Compatibility with TPU and other flexible polymers

Output geometry is directly suitable for FDM and SLS additive manufacturing as well as CNC milling from EVA or polymer blanks.

Validation

I validated the system by generating orthotics across a wide range of input profiles and evaluating geometric robustness under extreme parameter combinations, surface smoothness, consistency between generated designs, and expected load distribution behavior. The system consistently produced dimensionally accurate, structurally sound orthotics with no manual CAD adjustment required.
